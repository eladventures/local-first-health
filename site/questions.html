Questions to ask

Questions to ask before you sign or renew.

If you choose and pay for your clinic’s software, these are the questions we would ask first. You can ask them for a new contract, a renewal, or a decision to leave.

Start with one

  1. Can we keep a readable copy after our subscription ends?

It is a simple way to start finding out whether keeping access to what you wrote depends on keeping the subscription.

These are LFH's proposed questions. They have not been tested with clinicians. LFH does not rate, certify or approve software.

Then three more

  1. For how long after we cancel can we still get our copy, and is there a fee?
  2. What is in our copy, and what will we need to open it?

    Ask about attachments, images, scanned documents, and any notes, transcripts or recordings made with AI tools.

  3. What happens to our records if you close or another company buys you?

Ask whether they can show you how you would open a sample copy after cancelling.

Paying for software or support should never be a condition of retrieving existing records. Moving records into another system may need additional work. That work is different from getting your existing records back.

Getting an answer

We think a clear answer in writing is worth more than a spoken reassurance, and easier to check later. The questions are worth asking again at each renewal.

These questions are a starting point for a conversation with your provider, not legal advice.

Going further: questions by principle

Four of the five proposed principles end with a question you can ask a software provider. The fifth, testing with the people doing the work, is a question for builders; see For builders below.

  • Keep records usable when people change tools. Can I retrieve every existing record without a fee, and what would another tool need to use it?
  • Support essential work offline. If the internet goes down at 10 a.m., what can my clinic still do, and what has to wait?
  • Make records useful across care settings. If I send this record to another clinic, can their software use it, and can they see who wrote it and when?
  • Publish the foundations for others to build on. If you stop supporting this software, what would we need to keep reading our records, and who could maintain the software?

If an AI tool helps write your notes

If an AI tool helps write your notes, the same question applies.

  • Are our records, notes or recordings used to train AI models, and can we say no?

Tell us what you heard

If you asked any of these questions, we would like to hear what answer you got, and which question was hardest to get answered. We have limited capacity and may be slow to respond.

If you fund or buy software for clinics

These questions may help when you write your own requirements for software that clinics depend on. They are proposals, not a standard or certification, and LFH does not assess or approve software. Adapt them to your program; they do not replace legal advice about your requirements or contracts.

If you write requirements, tell us whether you would put these in, and which you would add or drop.

For builders

These two questions are for people who build software, not for clinics choosing it:

  • Publish the foundations for others to build on. Is the code public, under what license, and who decides what changes?
  • Test with the people doing the work. Who tested this, where, and what did they say was missing?
